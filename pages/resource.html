CV Do's And Don't

Should do

Use a clean, professional layout with consistent fonts.
Tailor your CV to each job by highlighting relevant skills.
Use action verbs: "developed," "analyzed," "designed," "managed."
Quantify achievements: "Improved efficiency by 20%."
Proofread multiple times and ask a friend to review it.
Save and submit as a PDF to preserve formatting.

Don't

Do not use flashy colors, photos, or decorative fonts.
Do not include irrelevant personal details like age or marital status.
Do not exceed one page as a student.
Do not send the same CV to every employer without customizing it.

Common Interview Questions

Practice these questions aloud. Click to reveal tips on how to answer.

Use the Present-Past-Future formula: briefly describe what you are studying now, mention relevant past experiences or projects, and share what you hope to do in the future. Keep it to 60-90 seconds.

Connect the role to your skills and career goals. Mention something specific about the company or team that genuinely excites you, not just generic praise.

Pick a strength relevant to the job with a real example. For weaknesses, choose something genuine and explain the steps you're taking to improve it.

Use the STAR method: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Focus most of your answer on the specific actions you took and the positive outcome.

Always say yes. Ask about team structure, what success looks like in the role after 90 days, or what they enjoy about working there.

Quick Interview Tips

Dress one level more formal than the company's daily dress code.

Arrive 10-15 minutes early to settle in and stay calm.

Bring extra copies of your resume and a notepad.

Maintain eye contact and listen actively to every question.

Bring a notepad and pen to jot down key points during the interview.

Send a thank-you email within 24 hours of the interview.

It is okay to pause and think before answering a difficult question.
